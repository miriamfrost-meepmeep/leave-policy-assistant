OVERVIEW
1.) What is Paid Leave?
Paid Leave represents time that a Covered Employee accrues and can use to take paid time off
for any reason of the Covered Employee’s choosing.
2.) What is Paid Sick Leave?
Paid Sick Leave is paid time away from work that a Covered Employee may use in order to take
care of a number of conditions, such as recovering from an illness, taking care of an ill family
member or even taking a family member to a doctor.
3.) Who is a Covered Employee?
A Covered Employee means an Employee who works at least 80 hours for an Employer within any
120-day period while physically present within the geographic boundaries of Chicago (this
interactive map can be used to determine if a location is within Chicago city limits City of
Chicago::Zoning Website).
Once that threshold is met, the Employee shall remain a Covered Employee for the remainder of
the time that the Employee works for the Employer.
There is no distinction between part-time, full-time, temporary, or seasonal employees.

4.) Does the Chicago Paid Leave and Paid Sick and Safe Leave Ordinance apply to independent
contractors?
No. The Ordinance only applies to employees as determined pursuant to Internal Revenue Service
(IRS) guidelines. Whether an individual is truly an independent contractor is determined by the
“Economic Realities Test” that is used by the Fair Labor Standards Act: Fact Sheet 13:
Employment Relationship Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) | U.S. Department of Labor.
*Please note the Ordinance applies to all Domestic Workers (as that term is defined in MCC 6-
100) regardless of their status as an Employee or independent contractor. Domestic Workers
must still meet the 80 hours within 120-day threshold to be a Covered Employee.
5.) Who is an Employer?
An Employer means a person who gainfully employs at least one Employee.
6.) When can Covered Employees start using their Paid Leave under the Chicago Paid Leave and Paid
Sick and Safe Leave Ordinance (Ordinance)?
Covered Employees are entitled to begin using their Paid Leave on their 90th day of employment
or July 1, 2024, whichever is later.
7.) When can Covered Employees start using their Paid Sick Leave under the Ordinance?
Covered Employees are entitled to begin using their Paid Sick Leave on their 30th day of
employment or July 1, 2024, whichever is later.
8.) When does accrual of paid time off begin under the Ordinance?
Accrual for Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave begins upon the start of employment, not after an
employee becomes a Covered Employee by working 80 hours within a 120-day period.
9.) Does the Ordinance apply to Employees covered by collective bargaining agreements?
Employees covered by a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) that was in force on July 1, 2024,
are not subject to the provisions of the Ordinance. However, once the CBA expires, a union and
Employer can negotiate over the terms of Paid Leave and Paid Sick and Safe Leave, including
whether to waive the requirements of the Chicago Paid Leave and Paid Sick and Safe Leave
Ordinance. A waiver should be set forth explicitly in such agreement in clear and unambiguous
terms.
There are different ways to accomplish an effective waiver in a CBA. One example that OLS
recognizes for purposes of enforcement is as follows: “Waiver of Chicago Paid Leave and Paid
Sick and Safe Leave Ordinance: To the fullest extent permitted, this agreement shall operate to
waive any provisions of the Chicago Paid Leave and Paid Sick and Safe Leave Ordinance, MCC 6-
130, and shall supersede and be considered to have fulfilled all requirements of said Ordinance
as presently written and/or amended during the life of this agreement.”

10.) What notices must Employers provide to Covered Employees?
The Employer must provide the Chicago Labor Laws notice to all Covered Employees with their
first paycheck, annually with a paycheck issued within 30 days of July 1, and post in communal
areas at a workplace. Notices must be provided in English and any language spoken by
Employees that do not speak English proficiently. Notices in twelve languages have been
provided by the Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection on the Office of Labor
Standards website. Requests for additional languages can be made via e-mail at
BACPLaborStandards@cityofchicago.org.
11.) Can notices be transmitted electronically?
Yes, the notices mandated to be provided with Covered Employees’ paychecks can be transmitted
electronically. Likewise, notices can be posted physically in break rooms or other communal
areas at a workplace and can be displayed electronically on electronic bulletin boards and TV
monitors in communal areas. All physical notices must be scaled appropriately (no less than 8.5
X 11 inches for notices provided with paychecks, and no less than 11 X 17 inches for workplace
postings). While there are no specific size requirements for electronic notices, they should be at
least as legible as the physical notice requirements.
ACCRUAL
12.) How is Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave time accrued?
Employees must qualify for and accrue Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave. If an Employee works at
least 80 hours in any 120-day period in Chicago, the Employee qualifies for Paid Leave and Paid
Sick Leave and is categorized as a Covered Employee. For every 35 hours worked, a Covered
Employee accrues one hour of Paid Leave AND one hour of Paid Sick Leave. The Covered
Employee may accrue up to 40 hours of Paid Leave and up to 40 hours of Paid Sick Leave in any
12-month period unless the Employer sets a higher limit. The 12- month period starts when a
Covered Employee first starts to accrue Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave.
13.) Can Paid Leave or Paid Sick Leave be awarded in anything other than hourly increments?
No, Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave can only be awarded in hourly increments.
14.) Can an Employer use an accrual rate that tracks fractional portions of an hour?
Yes, the employer can track accruals fractionally, but Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave hours must
be awarded in hourly increments.

15.) If a Covered Employee works outside Chicago, can that Covered Employee still accrue Paid Leave
and Paid Sick Leave?
Hours worked outside Chicago do not count towards the accrual of Paid Leave or Paid Sick
Leave.
*Please note that hours worked outside of Chicago may be subject to the Illinois Paid Leave for
All Workers Act (PLAWA), or the Cook County Paid Leave Ordinance. If you work in Cook County
BUT NOT in Chicago, please direct questions regarding the Cook County Ordinance to the Cook
County Commission on Human Rights.
If you work in Illinois BUT NOT in Cook County or Chicago, please direct questions regarding
PLAWA to the Illinois Department of Labor.
16.) Are overtime hours (non-exempt employees) worked by an Employee counted for accrual purposes
under the Ordinance?
Yes, employees eligible for overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act should have their hours
counted for accrual purposes under the City’s 1 hour accrued for every 35 hours worked metric.
17.) Are overtime hours (exempt employees) worked by an Employee counted for accrual purposes
under the Ordinance?
No. For employees who are exempt from the overtime provisions of the FLSA, Paid Leave and
Paid Sick Leave accrue based upon a 40-hour work week, unless the exempt Employee’s regular
work week is less than 40 hours. In instances where there is evidence that the exempt
Employee’s regular work week is less than 40 hours, Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave accrues
based upon that regular work week.
USAGE
18.) At what increment can Covered Employees use Paid Leave?
A Covered Employee may use Paid Leave at a minimum of four-hour increments. An Employer
may choose to allow a Covered Employee to use Paid Leave in smaller increments.
19.) At what increment can Covered Employees use Paid Sick Leave?
A Covered Employee may use Paid Sick Leave at a minimum of two-hour increments. An
Employer may choose to allow a Covered Employee to use Paid Sick Leave in smaller increments.

20.) If a Covered Employee uses more than the minimum increments (2 hours for PSL, 4 hours for PL),
can an employer set fixed periods for further use?
Yes. The respective minimum increments only apply to the first four (4) hours of Paid Leave and
to the first two (2) hours of Paid Sick Leave in a day. An Employer may require that a Covered
Employee take subsequent time in other increments.
Example: An Employer may set fixed periods of 30 minutes or any smaller amount of time
for the use of accrued Paid Leave, Paid Sick Leave or PTO.
21.) What documents are required when an Employee decides to use Paid Leave?
An Employer may not require a Covered Employee to provide a reason for the use of Paid Leave
and may not require them to provide documentation or certification as proof or in support of the
use of Paid Leave.
22.) What documents are required when an Employee decides to use Paid Sick Leave?
An Employer may require certification after a Covered Employee uses Paid Sick Leave for three
consecutive workdays.
23.) Can an Employer require a Covered Employee to conﬁrm in writing that the Covered Employee used
Paid Sick Leave as permitted under the Ordinance?
Yes. An Employer can require the Covered Employee to confirm in writing that the Covered
Employee used Paid Sick Leave for permitted purposes. However, the Employer cannot require
the Covered Employee to provide documentation from a medical or service provider if the
Covered Employee did not use Paid Sick Leave for three or more consecutive workdays.
24.) Under what circumstances can a Covered Employee use Paid Sick Leave due to a medical issue in
the family?
A Covered Employee can use Paid Sick Leave when a member of the employee’s family is ill or
injured, or to care for a family member receiving medical care, treatment, diagnosis, or
preventative care. The employee can also use Paid Sick Leave when the employee’s place of
business is closed by order of a public oﬃcial due to a public health emergency or to care for a
child whose school or place of care has been closed. Finally, the employee can use Paid Sick
Leave if a family member is the victim of domestic violence or a sex offense.
25.) Who counts as a family member for the purposes of Paid Sick Leave?
A family member is a Covered Employee’s child, legal guardian or ward, spouse under the laws of
any state, domestic partner, parent, spouse or domestic partner’s parent, sibling, grandparent,
grandchild, or any other individual related by blood or whose close association with the Covered
Employee is the equivalent of a family relationship. A child includes not only a biological
relationship, but also a relationship resulting from an adoption, step-relationship, or foster care
relationship, or a child to whom the Covered Employee stands in loco parentis. A parent includes a
biological, foster, stepparent or adoptive parent or legal guardian of a Covered Employee, or a
person who stood in loco parentis when the Covered Employee was a minor child.

26.) Can a Covered Employee use Paid Sick Leave to care for adult children?
Yes. The Ordinance allows Covered Employees to use Paid Sick Leave for the care of a child,
regardless of age.
27.) Does a dentist or eye doctor appointment count as acceptable use of Paid Sick Leave?
Yes. Just like a medical appointment with a doctor, dentist and eye doctor appointments qualify
as “receiving professional care, including preventive care, diagnosis, or treatment of a medical
issue.”
28.) What rate of pay do we use to pay our tipped employees if they use Paid Leave or Paid Sick Leave?
The Employer shall pay at least the highest amount of the base hourly wage, the federal minimum
wage, the Illinois minimum wage or the FULL Chicago minimum wage.
29.) What rate of pay do we use to pay our commission-based employees if they use Paid Leave or Paid
Sick Leave?
The Employer shall pay at least the highest amount of the base hourly wage, the federal minimum
wage, the Illinois minimum wage or the Chicago minimum wage.
30.) When should the 90-day lookback period be used to determine the compensation rate for Paid
Leave and Paid Sick Leave?
The 90-day lookback period should only be used when a Covered Employee received a reduction
in pay in the previous 90-day period. Outside of this scenario, Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave
must be paid at the same rate and with the same benefits that the Covered Employee regularly
earns during hours worked.
31.) If a Covered Employee gets sick in the middle of a scheduled vacation, can the Covered Employee
use Paid Sick Leave?
An Employer can establish a policy whether to allow a Covered Employee to utilize Paid Sick Leave
while on vacation.
32.) Can an Employer require a Covered Employee to telecommute or work from home instead of using
Paid Sick Leave?
No. An Employer cannot require a Covered Employee to work from home or telecommute instead
of using Paid Sick Leave.

PAYOUT
33.) Does Paid Leave under the Ordinance need to be paid out upon an employee’s termination,
resignation, or retirement?
Depending on the size of the Employer (see above box), accrued Paid Leave may need to be paid
out upon separation of employment. This includes when a Covered Employee is discharged due
to misconduct or other performance-related issues.
At no time does the Ordinance mandate that more than seven days (56 hours) of Paid Leave be
paid at separation of employment. The seven days include 16 hours carried over from one beneﬁt
year to the next (if applicable), plus 40 hours earned in the following beneﬁt year.
If the Employer provides vacation days or paid time off (PTO), there may be additional payout
obligations under the Illinois Wage Payment and Collection Act. For further clarity, please see the
FAQ, or reach out to the Illinois Department of Labor directly at DOL.Questions@Illinois.gov.
34.) How is the size of an Employer determined?
For purposes of Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave, numbers of Covered Employees will be
aggregated if they are employed by members of a single unitary business group as deﬁned for
Illinois income tax purposes.
35.) Does Paid Sick Leave need to be paid out upon an employee’s termination, resignation, or
retirement?
Unless an applicable collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise, an Employer is NOT
required to provide ﬁnancial or other reimbursement for unused Paid Sick Leave to a Covered
Employee.

36.) Does Paid Leave that is accrued during the first 90 days of employment have to be paid out if the
employer-employee relationship ends (e.g. termination, resignation, etc.) before the Covered Employee
would have the ability to use accrued and unused time?
Note: The Ordinance states that Employers must allow Covered Employees to use their Paid Leave
no later than 90 days after the commencement of employment.
Yes, Paid Leave that is accrued and unused must be paid out in the scenario that has been
presented, with the below caveats in place:
 The individual would have to meet the definition of a Covered Employee (worked at
least 80 hours in any 120-day period)
o Example, if someone quits or is terminated one week into their job, they
would not be a Covered Employee.
 The Employer has 51 or more Covered Employees
o Small Employers (1-50 Covered Employees) do not have to provide payout
under the Ordinance.
37.) Can an Employer pay out Paid Leave annually, as opposed to rolling/carrying over up to 16 hours
from one year to the next?
An Employer may have a policy giving a Covered Employee the option to receive payment for
accrued and unused Paid Leave (up to 16 hours) in lieu of carrying over the time. The policy must
be optional and not mandated (the Covered Employee has the option to choose rollover or
payout). If a payout is chosen, the voluntary agreement between the Employer and Covered
Employee should be in writing.
CARRYOVER
38.) Does an Employer need to allow carryover, also known as rollover, of Paid Leave and Paid Sick
Leave?
Unless an Employer frontloads Paid Leave hours, Covered Employees are allowed to carry over
unused, accrued Paid Leave from one year to the next. Covered Employees are allowed to carry
over up to 16 hours of Paid Leave.
Covered Employees are allowed to carry over unused, accrued Paid Sick Leave from one year to
the next regardless of whether the time is frontloaded or accrued. Covered Employees are
allowed to carry over up to 80 hours of Paid Sick Leave.
Covered Employees must be allowed to utilize their carryover time in addition to accrued time in a
new year.

39.) Does the Ordinance establish a balance cap for either Paid Leave or Paid Sick Leave when
considering the carryover parameters/limits?
Under an accrual method, a Covered Employee may carry over up to 16 hours of accrued and
unused Paid Leave. A Covered Employee could earn an additional 40 hours, so it would be
possible to have up to a 56-hour balance.
For Paid Sick Leave a Covered Employee may have up to a 120-hour balance. To arrive at this
scenario, a Covered Employee would carry over 80 hours of accrued and unused Paid Sick Leave
from one year to the next, then earn an additional 40 hours to reach 120 hours total.
EMPLOYER POLICIES / ACTIONS
40.) Does an unlimited paid time off (PTO) policy comply with the Ordinance?
A fact-speciﬁc analysis of the PTO policy would need to be conducted to ensure compliance with
Ordinance. Factors to be considered would be reasonable access and ability to utilize at least 80
hours of PTO in a year, rate of pay for the PTO time that is used, and notiﬁcation and approval
policies for the usage of PTO time.
41.) May an Employer frontload Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave at the beginning of the year?
Yes, an Employer may frontload Paid Leave and/or Paid Sick Leave by giving a 12-month-period’s
worth of Paid Leave or Paid Sick Leave.
An Employer may make available Paid Leave and Paid Sick Leave “in proportion” or “pro-rata” to
their ﬁscal or calendar year.
42.) Can an Employer limit the Ordinance-required parameters to the number of hours guaranteed by
the Ordinance?
An Employer that grants its Covered Employees greater Paid Leave/Paid Sick Leave hours than
the Ordinance requires may limit the Ordinance-required parameters to the number of hours
guaranteed by the Ordinance.
43.) If an Employer provides Covered Employees with leave benefits that exceed the Ordinance’s
requirements, must the Employer maintain records?
Yes. Employers must maintain records documenting compliance with the Ordinance, including if
the employer complies with the Ordinance by providing even more benefits than what the
Ordinance requires.

44.) Can an Employer establish policies that dictate how far in advance Covered Employees request
Paid Leave?
Note: Employers can establish preapproval procedures to ensure business continuity. Said
procedures should be in writing and given to Covered Employees in the language they commonly
use.
Covered Employees should have the ability to request/give reasonable notice AT LEAST SEVEN
days before using their Paid Leave.
Example: A Covered Employee can request their child’s spring break week which occurs
on April 5th-9th on January 10, as the notice date is at least seven days before the use of
the Paid Leave.
Employers may not require a Covered Employee to provide more than seven days’ notice before
using Paid Leave
HOW TO FILE A COMPLAINT
I’m an Employee and I think my rights under the Paid Leave and Paid Sick and Safe Leave Ordinance
have been violated. How can I submit a complaint to the Oﬃce of Labor Standards?
You can submit a complaint several ways:
1. Call 3-1-1
2. Use the CHI 311 mobile application
3. Download and mail a complaint form to the Oﬃce of Labor Standards, Department of
Business Affairs and Consumer Protection, 2350 West Ogden Avenue 2nd Floor, Chicago, IL
60608. The complaint form can be found online at: Chicago.gov/LaborStandards.
4. Download and email the complaint to the Oﬃce of Labor Standards at
bacplaborstandards@cityofchicago.org.
Information on the complaint form should detail the basis of your complaint. The Office of Labor
Standards will review your complaint and contact you to assess whether an investigation should
commence.
